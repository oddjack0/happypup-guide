Potty Training on a Schedule

Potty training is not really about teaching your puppy to “hold it”. It is about being outside, in the right spot, at every moment their small bladder says go — and throwing a party when they do. Get the schedule right and the learning almost takes care of itself.

The three golden rules

  1. Go out often. Young puppies need a trip every 1–2 hours while awake, plus at predictable moments (below). A rough guide to bladder capacity in the daytime is one hour per month of age: an 8-week-old needs out roughly every two hours at most.
  2. Supervise or confine. When you cannot watch your puppy with both eyes, they should be in their crate or pen. Most accidents happen in the ninety seconds nobody was looking.
  3. Reward outside, immediately. The treat must arrive within seconds of finishing, out there on the grass — not back at the kitchen door five minutes later. Timing is the whole lesson.
A sample day for an 8–12 week puppy
WhenWhat happens
On waking (about 6:30)Carry straight out, toilet, reward, then breakfast

Mid-morningToilet, play session, toilet again, then nap

AfternoonTrips every 1–2 hours and after every play session or chew

OvernightExpect one (boring, lights-low) trip in the first few weeks

Every toilet trip, step by step

  1. Take your puppy on a leash, to the same spot, through the same door, every time.
  2. Stand boringly for up to five minutes. As they start to go, quietly say your cue word (“go toilet”, “be quick” — anything you can say in public).
  3. The second they finish: praise like they have won a prize and give a treat. Then a short play, or come back in — your choice, but the reward comes first.
  4. If nothing happens in five minutes, go back inside, supervise closely or use the crate, and try again in 15–20 minutes. Puppies often need a little movement to get things going, so a short lap of the garden can help.

Accidents: the calm protocol

Accidents are information, not crimes — usually they mean the schedule slipped or supervision did. If you catch your puppy mid-accident, make a mild startling sound (a gentle “ah-ah”, not a shout), scoop them up and finish outside, then reward. If you find a puddle afterwards, just clean it up: your puppy cannot connect your reaction now with something they did minutes ago, and punishing after the fact only teaches them to hide next time.

Clean with an enzymatic pet cleaner, which removes the smell markers that say “toilet here” to a dog’s nose. Then adjust the plan: an extra trip at that time of day, and tighter supervision for a few days. A written log makes these patterns obvious — most puppies have one or two danger times.

How long does it take?

With a consistent routine, many puppies are largely reliable by four to six months, though genuine bladder maturity takes longer and most trainers consider a dog fully house-trained closer to a year. Small breeds often take a little longer. Expect the odd regression during adolescence, illness, or changes in routine — go back to the puppy schedule for a week and it usually resolves.

When to see a vet

Book a vet visit if your puppy strains to urinate, produces frequent tiny puddles, has blood in their urine, drinks noticeably more than usual, or if a previously reliable dog suddenly starts having accidents. Urinary infections and other medical issues are common, treatable causes — rule them out before assuming it is a training problem.

Accident-response checklist

  • Caught in the act? Calm interrupt, outside, reward any finishing outdoors
  • Found it later? Say nothing to the puppy — just clean it up
  • Use enzymatic cleaner, not regular household spray
  • Write it in the log: time, place, what happened just before
  • Add one extra trip to the schedule at that time tomorrow
